Verification story (what makes these numbers trustworthy)

Layered, each layer independently authored:

1. Rust invariant tests: energy conservation under asymmetric efficiencies, SoC bounds, no-export, per-port + aggregate + site-cap power limits, bill identities, DR window boundary semantics, persistence chain identity + clamp accounting, determinism (byte-identical, two-process SHA-256, CSV-row-permutation invariance), adversarial-policy safety (NaN/inf/huge/duplicate setpoints), charger queueing/exclusivity. 2. Hand-computed goldens: every dollar derived on paper in test comments. 3. Randomized property sweep: 200 seeded scenarios x 6 policies; all invariants asserted per run; corpus-coverage counters prove the sweep actually hits discharge, contention, binding caps, target misses, chain clamps, and DR overflow. 4. Hand-written DR covered-slot table: the (start,end] convention anchored by a literal slot list written from wall-clock arithmetic, independent of the code. 5. Independent Python referee (tools/referee.py, stdlib only, in CI): re-derives every billing metric from raw outputs, checks all physics bounds from the trace, verifies the persistence chain, and re-simulates all six heuristic policies from scratch, requiring slot-exact agreement. Two implementations of the same spec, written separately, agreeing to 1e-9 across 51,840 slot-records per dataset. 6. Mutation testing: 17 deliberate bugs (window-convention flip, dropped efficiency terms, deleted no-export guard, swapped demand components, dropped depletion, reordered event processing, broken incentive gate, ...) — after closing the survivors, every mutant is killed by a named test. Spot-verified after the fix round.

Review rounds (independent Opus agents) and what they caught

R1 plan review: 25 findings (5 critical), all fixed with regression tests. Highlights: the V2B discharge budget could sacrifice departure targets when a DR window abutted departure (fixed: provably-safe surplus-only budget); out-of-horizon DR events paid full incentives and took the bill negative; overlapping DR events double-billed; NaN setpoints were applied twice producing negative charge columns; the site cap was advisory; V2B donors could be stranded on unidirectional ports; the persistence clamp silently manufactured energy (now reported per session as chain_clamped_kwh).

Referee catch during execution: on the lossy dataset the discharge budget was converted to building-side power without the discharge efficiency, dipping 0.197 kWh below the reserve — exactly the class of shared-convention bug the independent referee exists to catch.

R2 correctness audit: 18 findings (2 critical), all fixed or resolved. The big one: the engine rationed scarce headroom in CSV row order instead of the policy's emission order, violating the spec, breaking row-permutation invariance under a binding site cap, and disagreeing with the referee — invisible because no shipped dataset set a site cap. Fixed with canonical session ordering + emission-order arbitration; the lossy month dataset now carries a binding 75 kW cap. Also fixed: NaN inputs sailed through validation and were silently swallowed into an under-stated bill (now rejected, "validated, never repaired" is true); EDF/LLF starved trivially feasible sessions when building load exceeded the firm level (now a force-charge fallback, mirroring standard practice: service guarantee outranks the window, penalty is paid); the month dataset's V2B donor ran dry after day 3 (now the V2B variants bank off-peak, which is also what makes the V2B numbers above meaningful); plus validation gaps (arrival below floor, negative targets, duplicate charger ids, duplicate series rows) and referee robustness fixes.

R2 mutation audit: 8/14 initial kill rate, 6 survivors + 2 bonus findings, every survivor closed with a test empirically verified to kill its mutant (the audit itself verified each proposed test kills). The recurring failure mode it named: tests that pass "for the wrong reason" because their geometry never makes the guarded path bind. All named instances fixed.

ACN-Sim V2B extension plan (item 3)

Full plan in docs/ACNSIM_V2B_PLAN.md, from a source-level study of acnportal v0.3.3. Recommendation: a plugin package (acnportal-v2b) over unmodified acnportal, not a fork (the credibility claim stays auditable: "V1G core is bit-identical to released Caltech code; V2B is an additive layer"), and not upstream PRs (master has had no merge since 2023-11). Verified file-level change list (11 components, ~7 S + 6 M, est. 2-3 weeks): bidirectional EVSE/battery/EV subclasses, a network override for the no-export guard, a V2B interface with building load + DR accessors, series tariffs, mirrored policies, a scenario bridge reading openv2b directories, and an 8-experiment cross-validation matrix (X1-X8) from unit-convention parity up to full billing parity and an information-loss canary. Known ACN-side traps documented (a linear-feasibility path that under-constrains signed schedules, stock algorithms clamping negative pilots, EV-only peak accounting).
